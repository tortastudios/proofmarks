I got rejected from 47 jobs last year.

For six months, I applied everywhere. Startups. Agencies. Big tech. 47 rejections.

I realized rejection isn't a verdict. It's data.

Every "no" taught me something. One interviewer said my portfolio was too long. Another said I talked about my team more than myself. A third said, kindly, that I seemed nervous. All of them were right.

So I cut my portfolio in half and practiced until interviews felt boring.

Here's the kicker: application number 48 got the offer.

How it works

  1. Paste the prompt at the top into your agent.
  2. Open your page.
  3. Select a mark.
  4. Ask your agent for a rewrite.
  5. Accept the rewrite, or dismiss the mark.

More ways to use it

  • Auto-proof a whole page. Ask your agent to proof the page, and it drafts a rewrite for every mark while you watch. The decision model checks each draft first. Then review them: accept one at a time, or all at once. Your agent never accepts for you.
  • Check in CI. proofmarks check judges files, folders or URLs without opening a page. In GitHub Actions it marks the pull request's changed lines, and --fail-on fails the build at the number of marks you choose. CI guide
npx proofmarks check content --format github --fail-on 1

Privacy

  • Your writing leaves your machine only to be judged or rewritten. Judging goes through Torta's relay, which doesn't keep the passages, and rewrites go to the agent you choose.
  • Usage counts are off unless you turn them on. If you do, Proofmarks sends counts of the marks you keep or dismiss, never your writing, to tune the rules for everyone. See everything it sends.
  • No account, no key. Or set your own decision model key, and judging skips the relay.
  • This site counts visits and clicks without cookies, and not at all if your browser asks not to be tracked.
