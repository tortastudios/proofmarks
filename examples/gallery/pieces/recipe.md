# Brown Butter Miso Cookies

There's something truly magical about the moment butter begins to brown. The kitchen fills with a warm, nutty aroma, the foam settles, and those little golden flecks appear at the bottom of the pan. It's a small kitchen miracle, and it's the secret behind the best cookies I've ever made.

I first had a miso cookie at a tiny bakery in Portland three winters ago. I was skeptical. Miso, in a cookie? But one bite changed everything. The miso doesn't make the cookie taste savory, it makes it taste more like itself: deeper, saltier, more caramel than sugar. I spent the next month testing versions at home until my roommate begged me to stop.

This is the version I landed on. The edges are crisp, the centers are chewy, and the flavor is the perfect balance of sweet and salty. Whether you're baking for a holiday party, a bake sale, or a quiet Sunday afternoon, these cookies are sure to impress.

## Why This Recipe Works

- **Brown butter** adds a rich, toasty depth that plain melted butter can't match.
- **White miso** brings a gentle saltiness and an almost butterscotch-like flavor.
- **Resting the dough** for at least an hour lets the flour hydrate, which gives you thicker cookies with better texture.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup (226 g) unsalted butter
- 1 cup (200 g) light brown sugar, packed
- ½ cup (100 g) granulated sugar
- ¼ cup (70 g) white miso
- 2 large eggs, at room temperature
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 2¼ cups (280 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 8 ounces (225 g) dark chocolate, chopped
- Flaky salt, for finishing

## Instructions

1. Melt the butter in a light-colored saucepan over medium heat. Keep cooking, swirling often, until it smells nutty and the solids at the bottom turn golden brown, about 5 to 7 minutes. Pour it into a large bowl, scraping in the browned bits, and let it cool for 15 minutes.
2. Whisk both sugars and the miso into the butter until smooth. Add the eggs and vanilla and whisk until glossy.
3. Stir in the flour and baking soda until just combined, then fold in the chocolate.
4. Cover the bowl and refrigerate for at least 1 hour, or up to 2 days.
5. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Scoop the dough into 2-tablespoon balls and space them 3 inches apart on lined baking sheets.
6. Bake for 11 to 13 minutes, until the edges are set and the centers still look slightly underdone. Sprinkle with flaky salt right away.
7. Let the cookies cool on the sheet for 10 minutes before moving them.

## Tips

Don't skip the rest. I know it's tempting, but unrested dough spreads into thin, flat cookies. If you only have time for one thing, make it this.

White miso is milder than red or yellow miso. If all you can find is a darker miso, use about two-thirds as much.

These keep for four days in an airtight container, though in my experience they never last that long. Happy baking!
