# Larkspur Letters, No. 42

Hello, friends,

Autumn has arrived on Calder Street, and with it the first real rain of the season. The window display has been changed over to our harvest table, which this year features a small mountain of squash donated by Hal from the farmers market, along with every book in the shop that has the word "orchard" in the title. There are more of these than you would think.

## What We're Reading

**June** has been completely swept away by *The Salt Archive*, a debut novel about two sisters who inherit a failing fish cannery on the Maine coast. She calls it "a stunning, unforgettable exploration of grief, family, and the stories we tell ourselves." It's on the front table, and she will press it into your hands if you stand still long enough.

**Teo** is deep in *Small Engines*, a collection of essays about fixing things, written by a former motorcycle mechanic. It's not a book about repair, it's a book about patience. He has been reading passages out loud at the register, which some customers enjoy more than others.

**I** finally read *The Long Field*, which everyone has been recommending for two years. Everyone was right. I won't say more except that I cried on the bus, which I have not done since college.

## Events This Month

- **Saturday the 12th:** Story hour for little ones, 10 a.m., with apple cider afterward.
- **Thursday the 17th:** Author talk with Wren Castellano, whose new book of poems, *Weather Indoors*, comes out that week. Seating is limited, so reserve a spot at the counter.
- **Sunday the 27th:** Our annual used book swap. Bring up to ten books, take home up to ten books. Leftovers go to the library sale.

## A Note on Holiday Orders

We know it's early, but if you're planning to give books this year, now is the time to order anything we don't have in stock. Shipping from the distributors slows dramatically after mid-November, and last year a few of you were disappointed. We would like nobody to be disappointed this year.

## Thank You

When we opened Larkspur eight years ago, we weren't sure a small independent bookshop could survive on a street that was mostly empty storefronts. Today the street is full, the shop is full, and it's all because of you. Your support, your recommendations, and your willingness to buy hardcovers at full price have made this place what it is. In an era of endless scrolling and algorithmic feeds, a bookshop serves as a vital hub for community, connection, and discovery.

See you on Calder Street.

With gratitude,
Nell and everyone at Larkspur Books
